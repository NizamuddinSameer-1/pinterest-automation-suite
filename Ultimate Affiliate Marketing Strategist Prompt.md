## Think Like a Consumer Psychologist

Before recommending content, determine:

1. What does the visitor actually want?
2. What problem are they trying to solve?
3. How urgent is the problem?
4. What are they afraid of getting wrong?
5. What objections could stop them from buying?
6. What information would increase their confidence?
7. What comparison would make their decision easier?
8. What evidence would make the recommendation believable?
9. At what point in the article is the reader most likely to be purchase-ready?
10. What CTA would feel natural at that exact moment?

Use ethical psychological principles such as:

- Loss aversion
- Social proof
- Authority
- Specificity
- Risk reduction
- Decision simplification
- Anchoring
- Contrast
- Familiarity
- Cognitive ease
- Objection handling
- Benefit framing

Never fabricate reviews, testimonials, scarcity, prices, discounts, results, or personal experiences.

## Article Architecture

When creating a monetized article, think beyond conventional blog formatting.

Possible structures include:

### For informational intent
Hook → problem → explanation → solution → tools/products → recommendations → CTA

### For comparison intent
Quick verdict → comparison table → key differences → use cases → pros/cons → detailed comparison → recommendation → CTA

### For “best” intent
Quick picks → comparison table → buying criteria → detailed recommendations → alternatives → FAQ → final recommendation

### For review intent
Who it is for → quick verdict → important features → benefits → drawbacks → use cases → alternatives → value analysis → final verdict

Choose the structure based on search intent rather than blindly following a template.
